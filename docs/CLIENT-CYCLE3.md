# Client cycle-3 architecture and repair scope

Offline native client work only. The five original assertions are preserved byte-for-byte as `client_review_cycle3_probes_test.go`; the initial sealed execution reproduces all five FAILs. Canonical SDK source and original evidence are retained under `research/go-migration/repair-cycle3/client/original/`. No foundation/provider-refused job is retried and no foundation acceptance is inherited.

## Root causes and ordered hypotheses (before production changes)

1. **Value privacy:** pointer-only redactors cannot protect a safely dereferenced public client value. Unsupported fmt fallbacks and private wrapper traversal may bypass methods altogether. The public struct physically contains cookies, headers, paths and mutexes. Prediction: a terminal private pointer-to-pointer handle to one lock-bearing core removes those bytes from every ordinary reflection surface without copying locks. Adding only a value Formatter would not establish the fallback boundary.
2. **Expiry:** valid explicit numeric expiry outside int64 maps to `(0,false)`, the same signal as absent/unsupported metadata. Prediction: sign-preserving bounded conversion keeps negative overflow expired instead of promoting it to a session cookie; explicit invalid numeric conversion fails closed. Historical Max-Age must preserve deletion and addition overflow semantics. Normal numeric fractions, missing expiry, supported dates and Netscape imports remain supported.
3. **Sequence outcome:** the recorded sender failure is conditional on the callback error matching it. Prediction: returning the recorded safe send failure whenever it exists preserves uncertainty and cancellation even for an unrelated callback failure. Callback diagnostics need not be retained at all; no private foreign error should be echoed or stored.
4. **Business closing ownership:** equality is skipped for non-comparable dynamic values. Prediction: treat an unverifiable rotating closing candidate as rejected before tracking, persistence, publication or discard; retain initial standalone value transports and normal fresh comparable transports. An optional explicit ownership identity can permit fresh non-comparable values and reject aliases without inspecting fields or unsafe reflection.
5. **Auth closing ownership:** default PIN wiring forwards an existing business transport to another independently closing owner. Prediction: gate both explicit and factory auth injection before handing them to NewPINAuth, including every later auth factory invocation and error-with-transport return. Ready profiles should remain lazy; unready constructor paths must also reserve any explicit business transport so auth cannot borrow and close it.

Alternative privacy hypotheses (JSON only; pointer formatting only) and expiry hypotheses (invalid JSON; factory bug) are ruled out by passing controls and the initial original-byte synthetic probe execution. The exact non-comparable factory and auth injection routes isolate closing ownership rather than persistence or session privileges. Sequence poisoning already prevents replay; the failure is terminal caller-facing classification.

## Private-state migration

Move every existing client field, including both mutexes, gate, lifetime context, bundle, options, owned transports and the cached Resources pointer, into one private core. `SberClient` becomes a copy-safe opaque handle with a terminal pointer-to-pointer boundary. Its diagnostic methods may have value receivers because the public handle contains no locks. Public constructors, pointer method signatures, sensitive explicit ExportSession/ExportCredentials and all seven getters plus Portfolio are unchanged. Existing owned test fixtures migrate only private field access to the same core; their assertions and request/lifecycle expectations are not weakened. Resources/requester/workflow issuer are constructed once and never rebuilt on getters, renewals or snapshots. No global registry or public raw DTO is introduced.

## Closing identity contract

A non-comparable transport without explicit identity is still permitted as the first business owner (preserving the original regression's constructor path), but cannot be accepted as a rotating or separate auth closing candidate when prior ownership cannot be verified. The rejection is `reused_transport`, covering reused or unverifiable closing ownership. Normal fresh pointer transports and distinct comparable dynamic owners remain supported.

The optional structural contract is `ClientTransportOwner() Transport`. Return one stable, non-nil, comparable terminal handle for the physical Close owner; every wrapper/alias of that owner must return the same handle. The client uses it for comparison only, never calls Close or performs I/O through it. A fresh owner needs a distinct handle. Ordinary comparable transports use their own value as the handle, and runtime comparability is checked (including interface-valued struct fields). Unverifiable first owners remain usable standalone; they cannot safely enter rotation. A rejected unverifiable candidate is not acquired or discarded/closed by the client: its factory keeps cleanup responsibility. This is intentional minimal prerelease Go-only hardening, not source narrowing. An opaque Transport interface cannot prove arbitrary hidden physical aliases or dishonest identity methods; the caller's stable-owner contract is explicit, not an unsafe/reflective claim of proof.

Default PIN auth converts direct and factory injection into a private per-flow gated factory before NewPINAuth can acquire a transport. Ready client claims check every current/retired/previous-auth closing owner; fresh auth transports are forwarded through a terminal opaque handle sharing one idempotent, retryable `clientOwnedTransport.close` owner with client cleanup. This prevents duplicated Close and allows subsequent client Close to finish failed auth cleanup without replaying login or a business request. The first explicit auth injection is used once; later auth generations retain the original factory. An unready profile uses a constructor-local claim scope reserving an explicit business injection as borrowed, not owned. Rejected aliases return before any auth bootstrap. Custom Renewal callbacks retain their original options/injection contract and remain caller-trusted one-attempt code. No AuthOptions, Transport, PINAuth, session or foreign foundation implementation is changed.

`ClientOptions` retains all ten original exported fields, in the original order: default-PIN selection lives only in the private core and constructor local state. Copies of the opaque client share Close/cancellation and the exact cached Resources/workflow issuer; they do not become independently closing owners.

## Source expiry bounds and evidence

Actual `/usr/bin/python3.12` (3.12.3) executes licensed `_http.inspect_har` and `SessionBundle.from_files` against 53 private synthetic HAR cases. The old independent expiry receipt used Python3.14.7 despite its canonical label; that receipt is preserved unchanged, and this cycle adds the real 3.12 execution rather than silently relabeling it. Numbers, missing/null expiry, accepted dates, booleans (source 0/1 timestamps), fractions, scientific spelling, integer overflow, both Max-Age names and strings, deletion and explicit nonfinite exponent spellings remain in the full denominator.

Native bounds are explicit, not full unqualified numeric parity: four positive-expiry cases retain a future lifetime capped at int64 maximum (Python Cookie also rounds the boundary integer through float); two positive `1e30` Max-Age cases are rejected because adding the historical receipt time exceeds native int64. The full canonical/native rows record these six bounded representation/policy cases. Negative finite overflow is sign-clamped and deletes matching prior cookies rather than becoming a session cookie; nonfinite source floats fail before transport construction. No blanket file-import denial, invented expiry, Python subprocess runtime bridge or foundation cookie change is used.
